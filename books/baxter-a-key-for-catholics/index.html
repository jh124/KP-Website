The book

About this edition

First published in 1659, A Key for Catholics by Richard Baxter (1615-1691) is one of his most robust works against the Romanists. The work is comprised of two parts. In part one, Baxter presents a series of arguments against the claims of the church of Rome. Drawing from Scripture, the Church Fathers, and History, Baxter addresses questions and claims surrounding apostolic succession, papal supremacy, and the nature of the Church. In part two, Baxter focuses his attention on the unity of the Church. In particular, he demonstrates that ecclesiastical unity is not defined by allegiance to a particular ecclesiastical body, but Christ and Him alone.
